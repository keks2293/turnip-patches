# 41. The fdperf configs are budget splits, not duplicates; ten counters have never had a reading, and two slots have selected the wrong counter since they were written (23.11)

One entry of this repository's journal. Index: [`docs/analysis.md`](../analysis.md) (generated); rules
for reading and writing: [`../../AGENTS.md`](../../AGENTS.md). Cite this entry as `journal N` or
`journal N.M`, where the number is also the start of the file name.

Bottom line: the fifteen `tools/fdperf/fdperf.cfg*` the audit read (sixteen counting `fdperf.cfg-gaps`,
which it then produced) cannot be merged into one config, because the union of what they ask for exceeds the hardware selector budget of eight blocks (UCHE 29 names for 12 slots,
VPC 19 for 6, TSE 10 for 4, PC 14 for 8) - they are different ways to spend a budget, and the part worth
centralising is the *selection*, which is now a script and one new config; 22 config lines sit past a
budget and will never be read, ten distinct names among them have no reading in any of the 72 committed
dumps, and a further check of names against ids found that `PERF_SP_GM_LOAD_LATENCY_CYCLES` has never
been selected at all, because the two configs that name it write id 83 where the a7xx enum puts 82.

## 41.1 The audit: what the budgets are, what is lost, what has never been measured

Asked on 23.11, from outside: the cfg list looks duplicated, many configs exceed the selection-counter
budget, and the duplication makes maintenance painful - the proposal was to merge them into one master
config and maintain the cautions in one place. Both halves were checked rather than agreed with, and the
rule the claim rests on was already in this repository as prose: `docs/fdperf-a7xx.md`, section "hw
counter budgets and silent ignore in fdperf", says fdperf reads only `counter0..num-1` per block and
drops the rest *without a message*, and lists what `fdperf.cfg` lost that way. Prose cannot be re-run, so
it is now a script.

**Method.** `scripts/fdperf-cfg-check.py`, with the per-block budgets committed as
`tools/fdperf/a7xx-budgets.json` (regenerated by `--update-budgets` from
`../mesa27-src/src/freedreno/registers/adreno/a7xx_perfcntrs.json`, provenance in the file's own `_note`).
For each cfg it parses every `counterN = id;` line, splits the lines of a block at `N >= num`, and - with
`--dumps` - takes the name out of the line's own comment and looks for that name in the headers of
`results/**/fdperf-raw.txt`. A name in no dump has never been selected on this machine; that is a
different claim from "measured as zero", and it is the claim three earlier entries had to make by hand.
Output: [`../results/fdperf-cfg-budgets.txt`](../results/fdperf-cfg-budgets.txt). The budget for 23.11 is
the reading of committed files; the sampler itself, `../build/out/fdperf`, is built from the turnip tree
and not vendored here, so the *rule* remains what the doc records as measured, not something re-derived
from source.

**Numbers.** Budgets (`a7xx_perfcntrs.json`, `groups[].num`), the blocks the cfgs actually use:
CP 14 (with id 0 marked `reserved`), SP 24, UCHE 12, TP 12, PC 8, VFD 8, RB 8, HLSQ 6, VPC 6, RAS 4,
TSE 4, LRZ 4, CMP 4, UFC 4, CCU 5, RBBM 4, VSC 2; the `BV_*` twins 7-8 each. The doc's "CP=13" is this
figure minus the reserved id 0; nothing in the tree depends on the difference, the highest CP slot any cfg
uses is 11.

| question | reading |
|---|---|
| lines past a budget, all cfgs | 22 |
| of those, with no reading in any committed dump | 10 distinct names, lost in 11 places (`PERF_CMPDECMP_VBIF_WRITE_REQUEST` is past a budget in two cfgs) |
| blocks where `fdperf.cfg` overruns | UCHE 19/12, PC 10/8, RBBM 6/4, CCU 9/5, LRZ 7/4, CMP 7/4 - the six the doc named, confirmed |
| the other fourteen cfgs | within budget everywhere except one line: `fdperf.cfg-traffic` puts a 5th
request counter in CMP's 4 |
| union over cfgs vs one budget | UCHE 29/12, VPC 19/6, TSE 10/4, PC 14/8, CCU 9/5, LRZ 7/4, CMP 7/4, RBBM 6/4 over; CP, SP, TP, VFD, RB, RAS, HLSQ, UFC, VSC fit |

The ten names with no reading anywhere: `PERF_UCHE_EVICTS`, `PERF_UCHE_CCHE_TPH_QUEUE_FULL`,
`PERF_UCHE_CCHE_DPH_QUEUE_FULL`, `PERF_PC_VPC_PRIMITIVES`, `PERF_RBBM_PC_DCALL_BUSY`,
`PERF_RBBM_PC_VSD_BUSY`, `PERF_LRZ_LRZ_WRITE`, `PERF_CCU_2D_RD_REQ`, `PERF_CCU_2D_WR_REQ`,
`PERF_CMPDECMP_VBIF_WRITE_REQUEST`. Four of them are things this repository has argued around without:
`PC_VPC_PRIMITIVES` is the denominator 40.33 could not find for the pixel side (its substitute
`PC_TAG_CHECKED_VERTICES` is programmed by no committed dump and reads 0 in the one run that asked for it,
40.35, whose mesh of 24576 independent triangles has no vertex reuse to check - see the correction at the
end of this section), `UCHE_EVICTS` is the only direct eviction counter on the block while the "L2
caches ~73 % of the vertex bytes" figure is an inference from what does not reach DRAM, and the two
`*_QUEUE_FULL` are the only client-side back-pressure counters on UCHE while the traffic summary quotes a
fixed ~220 ns DRAM latency. `scripts/fdperf-analyze.py` already *has* columns for three of them
(`RBBM_PCDC`, `RBBM_PCVSD`, and the SP latency pair below) and those columns are empty in every window of
every committed table - `results/games/re4/mesa27-vertex/fdperf-per-window.tsv`, 46 windows, all empty -
which is the right behaviour (the analyzer prints nothing when a name is absent) and the wrong appearance:
an empty column and a zero look the same to whoever reads the table next.

**Verdict on the proposal.** Merging is arithmetically impossible for eight blocks, so the cfgs stay. What
was true in the claim is that the *selections* were never revisited as a set and the cautions are
copy-pasted: the note that `VPC id 10` and `SP id 48` measure one signal is in six cfg files, and the
budget rule in four. Two things followed. `tools/fdperf/fdperf.cfg-gaps` allocates exactly those ten names
inside the budgets (UCHE 7/12, RBBM 3/4, CCU 3/5, CMP 3/4, PC 2/8, LRZ 2/4, CP 2/14), keeps a denominator
in every block, and is **NOT RUN** - its first job is to find out whether `EVICTS` and `*_QUEUE_FULL` move
at all, since the XML describes no unit for them and `UCHE_GMEM_*_BEATS` is the precedent for a counter
that reads 0 forever. And the budget rule became a checked assertion instead of a paragraph.

**Does not show.** Whether any of the ten counters means anything on this chip (nothing was run); what
the unit of any of them is; and what fdperf does with a *gap* in the slot numbering (`counter0`,
`counter2` with no `counter1`) - the script reports gaps as a fact about the file, not about the hardware,
because the binary is not vendored and no dump here was produced by a cfg with a gap.

## 41.2 Two slots have selected the wrong SP counters since the cfgs were written

Found by the same cross-check from the other direction: the script also lists, per cfg, the names it asks
for that no dump carries. For `fdperf.cfg` and `fdperf.cfg-vertex` that is exactly one name,
`PERF_SP_GM_LOAD_LATENCY_CYCLES`, although both cfgs have it at `counter9` inside SP's budget of 24.

The ids, from `../mesa27-src/src/freedreno/registers/adreno/a7xx_perfcntrs.xml:639-641`:

```
82  A7XX_PERF_SP_GM_LOAD_LATENCY_CYCLES
83  A7XX_PERF_SP_GM_LOAD_LATENCY_SAMPLES
84  A7XX_PERF_SP_EXECUTABLE_WAVES
```

and both cfgs said `counter9 = 83; // ..._CYCLES` and `counter10 = 84; // ..._SAMPLES`. So slot 9 selected
SAMPLES and slot 10 selected EXECUTABLE_WAVES: the dumps agree with the ids and not with the comments -
`grep -rh -o "PERF_SP_EXECUTABLE_WAVES" results --include=fdperf-raw.txt | wc -l` gives 297 readings, and
no dump carries `..._LATENCY_CYCLES`. The neighbouring pair (`119`/`120`, texture-fetch latency) is
numbered correctly and both halves are present in the dumps, which is why the mistake survived: the
latency *ratio* one would compute from GM load (`scripts/fdperf-analyze.py:77`, `LATENCY` entry `SP_GM`)
just came out empty, like the three columns of 41.1, and an empty column is not a number one argues with.

**Blast radius, checked before and after the edit**: no document in this repository quotes a GM-load
latency (`grep -rn "GM_LOAD\|SP_GM" docs/` reaches only cfg comments and the analyzer's table), so no
conclusion changes and no committed dump is wrong - the dumps carry the *true* names of what was selected;
it was the cfg comment that lied. The cfgs are corrected in this commit (`82`/`83`, with EXECUTABLE_WAVES
moved to its own slot 14 so the old dumps stay comparable), and the correction is written into both files
as an annotation, because a cfg that produced committed data should not silently change underneath them:
runs before 23.11 have no GM-load latency, runs after do, and whether id 82 moves at all is unmeasured.

## 41.3 The "UBWC cannot be checked with counters" passage was stale

The same audit made a second thing visible, because the cross-check reports `..._UBWC_READ_BEATS` as
"measured by another cfg: `results/games/re4/tr-base`". `docs/fdperf-a7xx.md` says in two places that the
UBWC counters "never fit in the budget" and that "the state of UBWC compression from hw counters on this
chip has not been verified". That was true of the two cfgs written on 17-21.11 and was overtaken by
`fdperf.cfg-traffic`, which spends UCHE slots 6-9 on exactly those counters, and by the 22.11 run
`results/games/re4/tr-base`, which 40.17 then used to answer open question 15.

Recomputed from the dump, as a check that the two records mean the same thing
(`scripts/fdperf_sections.py`, 51 windows, clock pinned at 680.02 MHz): `PERF_UCHE_UBWC_READ_BEATS`
median 19.797 M/s and `..._WRITE_BEATS` 2.252 M/s, non-zero in 51/51 windows; at 16 B per beat that is
316.8 MB/s and 36.0 MB/s, against 48.197 M/s `..._READ_REQUESTS_TP_UBWC` and 1948.88 M/s
`..._TP_NONUBWC`. 40.17's own row says 317 MB/s and 37 MB/s and the same request split, so the two
records agree and the sentence, not the measurement, was wrong. Both passages are corrected to point at
40.17 and the cfg that carries the counters. The part that stays true is the narrower one:
`UCHE_GMEM_READ/WRITE_BEATS` read 0 (they are absent from this dump and dead in the ones that carry them),
and neither `fdperf.cfg` nor `fdperf.cfg-vertex` can carry a UBWC counter inside UCHE's 12 slots - the
per-client set fills it, which is why `cfg-traffic` exists.

**What this entry is an argument for.** Three of the four defects here (a paragraph that outlived its
data, an id that selected something else for six weeks, a column that was always empty) were invisible to
a reader and visible to a check that reads config, dump and register XML against each other. That is the
cheapest kind of check this repository can own: no run, no GPU, minutes to re-run.

> Correction 23.11 (40.36): the sentence "its substitute, `PC_TAG_CHECKED_VERTICES` id 19, reads 0" named
> the wrong selector: id 19 is `PC_VERTEX_HITS`, which is alive in 2644 of 2645 windows that have it, while
> id 39 is `PC_TAG_CHECKED_VERTICES`. The claim itself - no committed reading of TAG_CHECKED - stands, and
> the "mesh where every vertex must pass the cache" premise is dropped with it: independent triangles share
> no vertices, so a zero there is the expected value, not a defect.
